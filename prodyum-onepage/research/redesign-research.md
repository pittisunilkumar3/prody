# ProDyum Website Design Research

## Recommendation

ProDyum should present itself as a cinematic creative studio with the restraint of a premium design practice. The site needs one compelling visual atmosphere, a concise explanation of its combined capabilities, and direct ways to explore those capabilities. An interactive website succeeds when an action produces a relevant and understandable change. A decorative object that rotates independently of the business story does not establish that relationship.

The recommended direction combines film-like imagery, generous editorial typography, quiet interface details, and a small number of content-driven transitions. Its purpose is to help a prospective client understand the studio, choose between a brand, digital, or film conversation, inspect the relevant services, and contact the team. The creative treatment should support that journey rather than require visitors to complete an animation sequence.

This is a design recommendation, not a measured conversion claim. No visitor analytics, customer interviews, validated client-project archive, or performance field data were available. The research combines primary studio references, the supplied reference repository, inspection of the existing ProDyum implementation, and primary accessibility and performance guidance.

## ProDyum content and audience

The existing content identifies two complementary businesses: ProDyum IT and ProDyum Entertainments. IT services include digital marketing, branding and design, web development, and video or multimedia. Entertainment services include films, original series, short films and music videos, and post-production. The supplied site content also links to three YouTube channels, invites careers and casting conversations, and gives hr@prodyum.in as the contact address.

These details are retained from the existing project content. The current prodyum.in homepage did not expose its content to text retrieval; it returned a JavaScript requirement. Consequently, this research does not independently reverify every business claim against the original live website. No awards, client logos, years in business, completed-project counts, staff profiles, or commercial outcomes should be added without evidence. [ProDyum](https://prodyum.in/)

The principal audience is a prospective client with a brand, digital, or production need. A secondary audience comprises creators, prospective teammates, cast, and crew. The main page should serve the primary enquiry journey first and preserve one clear collaboration entry for the secondary audience. An expansive simulation or hidden-navigation experiment would make that purpose harder to discover.

## Diagnosis of the previous design

The previous page used a lime-and-chrome hero, a moving typographic strip, repeated two-column sections, rounded service tags, a separate 3D knot, an amber film image, and a large lime contact panel. Those parts were individually functional, but they did not share a specific narrative. The chrome object suggested a generic technology brand; the amber image suggested filmmaking; the 3D knot had no meaningful relationship to either service list. The result read as a collection of effects.

The visual hierarchy also repeated its strongest gesture too often. Many headings used the same oversized sans-serif line followed by an italic serif line, while metadata and section numbers repeated around them. Large vertical spaces amplified the similarity. The proposed redesign varies composition while keeping type and imagery coherent: an immersive opening, a quiet studio statement, a changeable scene, a precise service index, a compact process, channels, and a direct closing invitation.

The animation implementation revealed another distinction: pointer tilt and a spinning object can be technically interactive while the page still feels passive. A more useful interaction lets a visitor choose a creative discipline and see its imagery, proposition, service context, and contact intent change together. Motion then explains a state change rather than simply demonstrating that the page can animate.

## Reference evidence

### Immersive Garden

Immersive Garden presents digital experiences through project-specific storytelling, including work for luxury brands and cultural subjects. Its homepage ties individual visual worlds to identifiable projects and provides a direct project index. The observed opening uses restrained typography and a largely monochrome environment; movement belongs to that environment rather than to a collection of independent cards. [Immersive Garden](https://immersive-g.com/)

The transferable principle is atmospheric consistency. ProDyum should use a coherent visual language and connect each major image to a business discipline. The reference's client names and project imagery must not be borrowed as evidence for ProDyum. Its work-first credibility cannot be reproduced by placing fabricated project names on generated images.

### Locomotive

Locomotive explicitly presents itself as a digital-first design agency, exposes Work, Agency, Careers, and contact navigation, and lists featured projects. It links design and code to the people and purpose behind the work. Its structure demonstrates that an expressive studio still benefits from clear identity, visible work categories, and straightforward next steps. [Locomotive](https://locomotive.ca/en)

For ProDyum, the applicable lesson is clarity of role and navigation. The first screen should say that the company works across brands, digital experiences, and films. A compact navigation system should remain understandable when the art direction is unusual. The distinctive typography of another agency is a reference for hierarchy, not a license to copy its identity.

### Hello Monday

Hello Monday groups its work into understandable categories such as branding, experiences, platforms, and products. Project links are central to the page, and business, general, and employment enquiries have distinct destinations. [Hello Monday](https://www.hellomonday.com/)

The relevant principle is that interaction can organize complexity. ProDyum's scene selector will group its offering into brand, digital, and film while preserving the detailed service lists underneath. It will not claim that conceptual artwork is a finished project. The contact subject will reflect the visitor's interest without pretending that a message has already been sent.

### UNIT9

UNIT9 gives experiential, interactive, and film disciplines direct prominence in its navigation and opening content. This is a useful reference for a studio whose capabilities cross digital and production boundaries. [UNIT9](https://www.unit9.com/)

The applicable design decision is to make the disciplines themselves navigable. ProDyum does not need an obscure poetic label for every business activity. Brand, digital, and film are recognizable entry points. The visual language may be cinematic while the controls remain literal and clear.

### Active Theory

Active Theory describes integrated in-house design and development across digital experiences. Its retrieved site includes an unsupported-graphics message because parts of the experience depend on WebGL. [Active Theory](https://v5.activetheory.net/)

The lesson is two-sided. A tightly integrated visual experience can convey creative capability, but core business content should not depend on graphics hardware. ProDyum's text, navigation, services, and email path should remain ordinary HTML, with animation layered over that foundation. Removing the standalone WebGL knot also avoids requiring the larger graphics runtime for an unrelated ornament.

### Supplied VOID ATLAS reference

The supplied repository describes a video-backed tunnel that becomes a star field with layered typography. It also explicitly documents limitations: the form is a frontend demonstration and some navigation links point to the same introduction. [VOID ATLAS repository](https://github.com/vikod3/void-atlasp)

The useful principle is continuous spatial storytelling. The important limitation is that a visual demonstration is not a complete business website. ProDyum should retain a coherent flow while using real section destinations and honest contact behaviour. Its identity should remain grounded in the studio's work rather than imitate a deep-space theme.

## Design synthesis

| Area | Existing weakness | Recommended treatment | Reason |
|---|---|---|---|
| Opening | Generic chrome-and-lime technology imagery | A cinematic landscape with a tangible terracotta sculpture | Establishes scale, atmosphere, and a more distinctive visual world |
| Typography | Many similarly oversized mixed-style headings | Deliberate contrast between display serif and precise sans-serif | Creates editorial variety without visual clutter |
| Colour | Lime, green-grey, chrome, and amber competing | Charcoal, near-white, terracotta, and image-derived warmth | Connects film and design imagery within one palette |
| Interaction | 3D ornament separate from content | Scene choices update imagery, copy, service context, and enquiry intent | Gives each input a useful outcome |
| Services | Accordion competing with an unrelated object | Readable service index with relevant imagery | Makes capabilities easier to compare |
| Motion | Several unrelated repeating effects | Image depth, one-time entrances, and short state transitions | Creates continuity without constant competition |
| Proof | Concept art can resemble portfolio work | Clearly distinguish studio concepts from actual channel links | Preserves trust while real project assets are unavailable |
| Contact | Large decorative close | A concise project invitation with an interest selector | Supports a concrete next step |

The proposed hero uses a shore, a human-scale silhouette, and a large architectural disc. This is a creative choice, not a researched claim about ProDyum's productions. The image serves as original studio concept art. The branding image uses folded red paper and glass in an architectural interior, linking physical craft to identity and interface design. Existing cinematic-stage artwork remains appropriate for the film discipline. None of these images is described as a commissioned client project.

## Single-page information architecture

The opening communicates the studio's central promise and immediately names its three creative areas. Its primary action leads to the interactive world selector. A studio statement follows, explaining how digital and entertainment capabilities belong to one team ecosystem. This is intentionally shorter than a full company-history section because the available content does not support a detailed history.

The scene selector is the main interactive moment. Three labeled buttons and previous/next controls let visitors explore brand, digital, and film. The selected scene changes the image, descriptive copy, and service destination. The interface should work with click, touch, and keyboard controls; it should not automatically advance while someone is reading. A horizontal gesture may supplement explicit controls, but must not replace normal vertical scrolling.

The service section preserves all eight existing service groups under the two business divisions. Opening a group reveals its description and deliverables. The content is accessible without a route change. A concise process section explains how a project moves from brief to direction, making, and delivery. These are descriptions of an intended workflow, not quantified performance promises.

The channels section retains the three existing external YouTube destinations. The collaboration line preserves career, casting, crew, and partnership enquiries. The closing contact area lets a visitor choose an enquiry topic and opens a pre-addressed email draft. No artificial success message appears, because there is no submission service behind that link.

## Motion and interaction specification

| Trigger | Visible response | Purpose | Constraint |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pointer over hero | Small image displacement and framing response | Gives depth to the cinematic opening | No text movement that makes reading difficult |
| Ordinary page scroll | Bounded image parallax and section entrance | Connects the visual narrative | Never intercept wheel or touch scrolling |
| Scene selection | Image transition and updated text/context | Explains the chosen discipline | No timed autoplay; selected control remains apparent |
| Service heading click | Expand/collapse associated details | Supports comparison and deeper reading | Proper expanded state and keyboard activation |
| Focus/hover on a link | Underline or directional movement | Makes affordances discoverable | Equivalent visible keyboard focus |
| Motion pause | Stops ambient image movement | Gives visitors control | Manual content selection remains functional |
| Reduced-motion preference | Removes nonessential spatial animation | Respects system preference | All content remains available |

W3C's technique for reduced motion explains that interaction-triggered animation can be suppressed according to user preferences. The redesign follows this approach for image movement and entrances. This does not establish full WCAG conformance; it addresses a specific motion behaviour. [W3C C39](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG21/Techniques/css/C39.html)

Google's animation guidance recommends favouring transform and opacity and examining the cost of properties that trigger layout or paint. These are the default properties for scene transitions and depth effects. Scroll work is scheduled with animation frames, and transformations are bounded. The design does not require a continuous loop to update static text. [web.dev animation guide](https://web.dev/articles/animations-guide)

Text over an image needs more than a chosen colour: the underlying photograph varies. A local dark gradient behind the hero and scene copy protects legibility while preserving bright image details elsewhere. Normal text should target a contrast ratio of at least 4.5:1; large text has a 3:1 threshold under the referenced criterion. Actual combinations still require inspection. [W3C Contrast Minimum](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/contrast-minimum.html)

## Performance and resilience

Core page content should render as HTML rather than inside a canvas. The page should not wait for a film download, a shader compilation, or an introductory loader before revealing its proposition. The hero image is prioritized; images lower on the page are deferred where appropriate. Existing package versions and hosting configuration remain intact, avoiding an unnecessary framework migration.

The main performance risk is large image payloads, particularly on mobile. Original assets should be retained in the project, while browser delivery can use compressed versions when practical. Another risk is reading-related layout movement as service panels open. Expansion should affect normal document flow so the page remains scrollable and does not cover adjacent content.

Responsiveness is part of perceived quality. Google describes Interaction to Next Paint as a measure of response latency across qualifying interactions. The site should therefore prioritize prompt tab selection and accordion updates over additional spectacle. No numeric INP improvement is claimed without measurement. [web.dev INP guidance](https://web.dev/articles/optimize-inp)

## Validation and remaining evidence gaps

The implementation review should cover desktop and narrow mobile layouts, section navigation, every scene choice, service switching, accordion expansion, enquiry-topic changes, reduced motion, image availability, and unexpected horizontal overflow. Scene imagery must remain meaningful when cropped vertically. Controls should keep enough space for touch and visible focus for keyboard use. Legacy URLs should lead to the appropriate part of the single page.

A polished interface cannot substitute for actual evidence of studio capability. The largest remaining content opportunity is a client-approved project archive: a few strong stills, project titles, roles, and concise case descriptions. When available, those can replace conceptual imagery in a genuine work section. Until then, the site should present its services and real channel destinations honestly.

The research supports a coherent implementation direction; it cannot guarantee subjective approval or commercial performance. The selected direction should be evaluated against three practical questions: can a visitor identify what ProDyum does immediately, can they find the relevant service without waiting for animation, and can they start a clearly described enquiry? Visual refinement should improve those outcomes while giving the studio a memorable identity.
